# Modeling-Social-Program-Co-Participation-Across-Territories-Using-Survey-Weighted-Ising-Networks

Households often participate in several social programmes. Their observed overlap, however, mixes household composition with dependencies among participation indicators. We analyse seven programmes in a processed ENAPRES 2025 extract covering 36,184 Peruvian households. Weighted symmetric Ising pseudolikelihood estimates pairwise conditional interactions, allowing 23 household controls. Sparse networks are selected by grouped cross-validation and assessed through fixed-penalty cluster resampling; unselected ridge coefficients are used for exploratory territorial Wald contrasts. A nested, cluster-held-out benchmark compares the adjusted Ising model with prevalence-only, unadjusted Ising and independent household-covariate models. Its weighted conditional log-loss is 0.2015 versus 0.2100 for the covariates-only model, a 4.07\% reduction. Of 15 national adjusted edges, 12 recur in at least 95\% of 75 resamples. Across territories, 24 of 84 pairwise contrasts pass within-family Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment, while just 12 coincide with a change in edge presence between the corresponding sparse graphs. Conditional interactions therefore carry information beyond household controls, and graph-topology changes do not substitute for parameter contrasts. Reported weights and incomplete survey-design metadata limit population-level inference.
